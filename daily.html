U.S. mortgage rates climb to their highest level since 2023

Daily CET-6 Reading · adapted from CNBC Select reporting, Oct. 2, 2026

U.S. mortgage rates have risen sharply, making home purchases more expensive for many prospective buyers. According to figures cited by CNBC, the average rate on a 30-year fixed mortgage reached 7.28% in the week ending Oct. 1, the highest level since November 2023.

The increase marks a significant trajectory shift from earlier expectations that borrowing costs would ease. Higher oil prices and persistent inflation have contributed to upward pressure on interest rates, while uncertainty in the global economy has complicated the outlook for borrowers.

Elevated mortgage rates can stymie housing demand because monthly payments become less affordable. For prospective buyers, even a modest change in rates can substantially alter the total cost of a loan. This makes it more difficult for households to plan their finances and may cause some buyers to postpone purchases.

Mortgage-rate data are also a useful retrospective indicator of broader financial conditions. They reflect thousands of conventional loans and help show how economic trends and major events have impacted the housing market. Although future rates remain uncertain, borrowers can compare offers and pay close attention to how inflation, energy prices and monetary policy affect financing costs.
